# Plan: Ata 2.2 and 2.3

Written 2026-10-20, the night 2.1.0 shipped. Decisions below were made without a back-and-forth;
each one says why, so any of them can be overturned in the morning.

## 2.2 — better transcripts, and a history you can see

### 1. A better local model

**Problem:** the user finds large-v3-turbo's transcripts poor. Turbo keeps large-v3's encoder
but cuts the decoder from 32 layers to 4; that is where accuracy on European Portuguese and on
noisy VoIP audio suffers.

**Plan:** a model picker instead of one hard-wired file, with the default chosen from measured
word error rates on real Portuguese and English speech (clean and telephone-degraded), not from
model cards. Decoding flags (beam search, context, prompt) are re-tuned on the same data.
Existing users are offered the better model with a one-click download; the old one keeps
working until the new one is in place, and unused models can be deleted from Settings.

### 2. Re-transcribe any meeting with any model

Right-click a meeting → **Transcribe again with…** → any installed local model or any OpenAI
model. The previous transcript is never overwritten: it moves to `versions/` inside the meeting
folder, named by date and model, so you can compare.

*Why a subfolder rather than a database:* every meeting stays a plain folder you can read,
sync and grep; nothing else in Ata needs a database, so neither should this.

### 3. What it cost

Every transcription records engine, model, audio minutes, and — for OpenAI — tokens and an
estimated price, in `meeting.json`. Prices come from a table in the code with an "as of" date,
because OpenAI's API returns token usage but not money. Local transcriptions cost $0, and the
history shows what the same audio *would* have cost on OpenAI.

### 4. The history, animated, in the menu

The menu gets a second tab, **Insights**, built with Swift Charts (part of the SDK — no new
dependency):

- hours recorded and meetings per week, as an animated bar chart;
- talk time, you vs. the others, overall and per meeting;
- words transcribed, money spent this month, and money saved by transcribing locally;
- which apps your meetings happen in;
- a timeline of transcriptions: every version of every meeting, with its model.

Numbers count up and charts grow in when the tab opens. Meetings transcribed before 2.2 are
backfilled from their `transcript.md`, so the history starts full rather than empty.

### Result of the evaluation (2026-10-21)

425 transcriptions on an M4 with whisper.cpp 1.9.5, all European Portuguese and UK English:
parliament speech, a spontaneous Braga sociolinguistic interview (CAMÕES), and a real AMI
meeting, each clean, VoIP-degraded (Opus 24 kbps + noise) and phone-degraded (300–3400 Hz,
Opus 12 kbps, more noise), plus silence-heavy and mixed-language files.

**The flags mattered more than the model.** Word error rate, %:

| | PT speech c/v/d | PT conversation c/v/d | EN speech c/v/d | EN meeting c/v/d | RTF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Turbo, 2.1 flags | 2.8 / 2.8 / 8.7 | 23.4 / 32.7 / 59.5 | 10.6 / 11.2 / 19.1 | 13.8 / 32.0 / 73.7 | 0.054 |
| Turbo, 2.2 flags | 2.4 / 2.0 / 5.3 | 24.0 / 30.0 / 57.1 | 10.4 / 10.1 / 14.2 | 13.8 / 22.7 / 67.3 | 0.054 |
| **Large v3 q5_0, 2.2 flags** | **1.7** / 2.1 / **4.1** | **21.8** / **28.5** / 57.2 | 9.2 / 10.6 / 13.4 | 13.1 / **20.9** / 67.2 | 0.18 |

- With text context on (whisper's default), every model sometimes fell into a repetition loop —
  up to 788 inserted words. `-mc 0` removed all of them.
- whisper.cpp's default VAD clipped the first words of short turns; `-vt 0.35 -vp 300 -vsd 500`
  fixed it (VoIP meeting 32.0 → 22.7).
- `--prompt` made loops more likely; no VAD at all produced 300-word loops in silence.
- Auto language detection: Large v3 heard UK English as Welsh at p 0.89–0.99, and short clips
  were misdetected 9 times out of 40. Hence detection per track, accepted only for the user's
  own languages at p ≥ 0.8.

**Shipped:** Large v3 (q5_0, 1.08 GB) as the default, Turbo and Turbo compact as options, the
flags above, and per-track language detection. Large v3 full precision was dropped (same
accuracy as q5_0 at three times the size), as was medium (worse and slower).

## 2.3 — knowing who spoke

### The idea

Today the system-audio track is one speaker, "Others". In 2.3:

1. **Split** each meeting's system track into speakers (diarization), on-device.
2. **Fingerprint** each speaker with a voice embedding.
3. **Match** those fingerprints against a local library of known voices across meetings.
4. **Ask once:** an unknown voice that keeps coming back shows up in the menu with a short
   sample to play — "Who's this?" — and you type a name.
5. **Remember:** from then on that voice is named in every transcript, past ones included,
   since re-labelling an old meeting is just re-matching its stored fingerprints.

### Decisions

- **On-device only.** Voice fingerprints are biometric data; they never leave the Mac, even
  when OpenAI transcribes. They're stored in `~/Library/Application Support/Ata/voices/`,
  can be wiped from Settings, and the feature is off until you turn it on.
- **Unknown voices are only surfaced after they recur** (seen in at least two meetings), so you
  aren't asked about every one-off guest.
- **Your own voice** is learned for free from the mic track, which also helps keep you from
  being mislabelled when your mic leaks into the system track.
- **Samples:** a few seconds of the clearest stretch of each speaker, cut from the kept audio.
  This is one more reason audio is kept by default.
- **Engine:** chosen after a proof-of-concept running on this Mac (two different voices
  recognised across two files) rather than from documentation. If the best option means adding
  the project's first dependency, that is the one dependency worth adding, and the README and
  CONTRIBUTING say so.

### Result (2026-10-21)

Built on **FluidAudio 0.17.7** after a proof-of-concept on this Mac: synthetic voices were
told apart and matched across files (same voice 0.95–0.96, different 0.02–0.49; 115× real time;
21 MB of models). sherpa-onnx was the alternative — workable, but no SwiftPM package and CPU
only.

What the end-to-end tests turned up that the plan didn't foresee:
- Whisper's segments run across speakers, so lines are split word by word at the turns.
- Whisper's word times lag the audio; changes snap to the nearest sentence end or pause.
- Per-meeting "Speaker 1, 2" gave the same person different labels in different meetings;
  voices now get a permanent number.
- In transcripts Whisper leaves unpunctuated, one word at a boundary can still land on the wrong
  speaker. Not solved.
